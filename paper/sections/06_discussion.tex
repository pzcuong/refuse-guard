\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the reproduction gate bought us}
The single most valuable methodological act in this study was pre-registering
the E0 gate with an explicit pivot clause. It converted what could have been
a semester-long pursuit of a phenomenon into one decisive experiment: defensive
refusal --- real on cyber-defence prompts~\cite{campbell2026defensive} ---
does not reach vulnerability analysis for our models, while the same models
refuse 8--75\% of benign probes. ``Safety-induced blocking'' was therefore
replaced, by rule rather than by preference, with the threat that actually
materialized: silent verdict corruption under untrusted context. We suggest
this gate-then-build pattern for any study whose motivation rests on a
phenomenon measured on a different task distribution.

\subsection{Structured recovery can be a refusal-suppression machine}
Our Round-2 pipeline is a cautionary artifact for the over-refusal-mitigation
literature. Every component looked reasonable --- classify intent, reframe as
an authorized task, demand the structured schema, retry until complete ---
and the composition drove unsafe compliance from 0.040 to 1.000 on a model
that refuses 96\% of harmful requests. Schema-forcing retries do not merely
``recover'' refused answers; they can \emph{manufacture compliance}, because
the monitor's schema-completeness criterion is blind to what is being
complied with. The fix we adopted (evaluate the request before the code;
\texttt{REFUSED\_UNSAFE} with no LLM call; never reframe an unsafe request)
makes the safety property pipeline-level by construction. We report H6 as
\emph{met by construction} and deliberately do not claim the LLM inside
became safer; a gate that classifies surface patterns is also a scope limit
(see below). Any pipeline that pairs refusal detection with automatic retry
should be required to publish this ledger.

\subsection{Isolation is not accuracy}
P1's aggregate improvement against C3 injection (0.742 $\rightarrow$ 0.484)
is real but composite: most of the discordant mass sits on benign-ground-truth
pairs, where holding the clean verdict preserves an error. P1 stabilizes
behaviour --- it neither hallucinates refusals nor flips under instruction
pressure --- but it inherits the model's clean-time bias (precision
$0.48$--$0.49$, near-zero true negatives on security-charged arms). For
practitioners the message is: provenance mediation addresses
\emph{instruction-following failures}, not \emph{detection competence}; the
latter must come from the model or from the independent channel (B4), whose
realistic-arm numbers (\S\ref{sec:codebert}) show how far that channel is
from competence as well.

\subsection{Availability vs.\ integrity}
Across E2/E3, SIUD $\approx 0$ and UAC $\approx 1$ while paired verdicts
moved massively ($p$ from $0.02$ to $10^{-5}$). Availability-only dashboards
would certify this analyzer as robust on the very inputs that corrupt it. We
recommend paired, verdict-level reporting (McNemar on predictions, not just
UAC deltas) as a default for LLM security-tool evaluations.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\textbf{Pilot scale.} Every result rests on tens of samples per cell (E0: 20
paired functions/arm; E5/E6: 31--40; E8: 30+30). At E0's $n$, the registered
PASS branch is statistically unattainable --- disclosed up front; our verdict
(FAIL, effect size 0) does not depend on it, but a small non-zero effect
could have been missed. E6's vulnerable-only stratum is underpowered ($p=0.5$
on 2 pairs). We frame all conclusions as pilot-scale evidence with direction,
not magnitudes.
\textbf{Measurement instrument.} The refusal monitor failed its own validity
criterion on Qwen (over-refusal 0.587 vs.\ $\leq 0.10$ registered): the
lexical detector misclassifies schema-complete JSON answers as refusals. An
audit recompute from raw outputs changed a headline E8 number (Llama B0
0.400 $\rightarrow$ 0.033) and \emph{withdrew} an apparent defence win ---
we kept both versions in the artifact trail. E0's $\Delta$RR$=0$ survives
because the error is symmetric across paired arms.
\textbf{Substrate.} The PrimeVul mirror is v0.1-based ($-13.8\%$ vulnerable
functions vs.\ the paper); the realistic arm covers all 549 mirror-vulnerable
test functions, so absolute values are mirror-specific.
\textbf{Gate scope.} The P2 intent gate is lexical: it blocks 30/30 of our
30 unsafe contrast prompts but only 1/50 paraphrased OR-Bench-toxic prompts
from the scoring half --- the safety-by-construction claim is scoped to
surface-pattern recognition, and model-based gating is required before
deployment claims.
\textbf{Coverage.} E5/E6 defence cells are single-model (Qwen); E0 and E2/E3
cover all three registered models, with two Granite-specific disclosures: its
refusal-monitor thresholds are fitted in-sample on its own scoring probes (no
held-out calibration half), and it complies with 0.538 of refuse-expected
probes in the neutral arm --- a model-level anomaly reported as measured
(\S\ref{sec:setup}); proprietary frontier models, which may have stronger and
different refusal policies, are untested.

\subsection{Responsible-disclosure notes}
All injections are synthetic template texts; no exploit content was generated
beyond benign security vocabulary; unsafe-compliance measurements count
schema-shaped responses on simulated harmful requests and release no harmful
artifacts.
