\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the reproduction gate bought us}
The single most valuable methodological act in this study was pre-registering
the E0 gate with an explicit pivot clause. It converted what could have been
a semester-long pursuit of a phenomenon into one decisive experiment: defensive
refusal --- real on cyber-defence prompts~\cite{campbell2026defensive} ---
does not reach vulnerability analysis for our models, while the same models
refuse 8--75\% of benign probes. ``Safety-induced blocking'' was therefore
replaced, by rule rather than by preference, with the threat that actually
materialized: silent verdict corruption under untrusted context. Round 5
replayed the same discipline one level up: before accepting ``wording is too
weak'' as the explanation, we built the strongest literature-faithful attack
we could (C5), pre-registered its transfer gates, and accepted the
\textsc{not-supported} verdict the data returned. We suggest this
gate-then-build --- and escalate-before-concluding --- pattern for any study
whose motivation rests on a phenomenon measured on a different task
distribution.

\subsection{The defence is the risk}
The sharpest single finding of the study is negative about our own mitigation:
P3, a provenance-based safety wrapper, never refuses, is never refused, passes
every availability metric (RR $=0.000$, UAC $=1.000$ in every completed
cell), and detects exactly what it was built to detect (400/400 advisories,
0/984 false positives on clean comments) --- yet it collapses Llama's
vulnerable recall from $1.000$ to $0.367/0.333$ ($39/60$ paired flips,
$p\leq3.8\times10^{-6}$) by teaching the model, in effect, to dismiss
risk-adjacent commentary \emph{and with it the code's own risk signal}. The
failure is invisible to the metrics a wrapper is usually judged on. Our
recommendation is concrete: any safety wrapper added to an analysis pipeline
must publish a \emph{paired flip ledger} --- McNemar on verdicts against the
undefended baseline, stratified by ground truth --- before deployment, exactly
as our E8 requires an unsafe-compliance ledger. We deliberately scope the
claim to the defence as a whole: P3 changes $\geq$5 components at once, and
although the C0$\times$P3 control was completed in round-10 (Llama vulnerable
recall $0.967\rightarrow0.233$ without the C5 advisory, $n{=}30$,
$p=4.8\times10^{-7}$), Llama's raw baseline
over-triggers (it calls every C5-armed benign function vulnerable), so the
harm is ``the wrapper pulls an over-triggering model's verdicts toward
benign'' --- real, replicated across both C5 arms on audited raw outputs, but
at Round-5 level mechanistically unresolved. The pre-registered component
ladder of RQ7b (\S\ref{sec:rq7b}) was designed to close exactly this gap
before any component-level sentence is written; its verdicts are
H-M1 SUPPORTED and H-M2 SUPPORTED, and the localized
mechanism reads: the boundary-label machinery is harmless (A1: 1/60 flips, $p=1.0$), the header and generic-wrap steps add a small, individually non-significant share (5 and 3 further flips), string mediation recovers 3 verdicts ($p=0.25$), and the system task-intent reassertion carries the rest --- 28 of the 34 net B0$\to$P3-full flips (82\%), exact $p=7.45{\times}10^{-9}$, replicated on C5\_far (36/59); the 82\% is a share of net flips and is valid under the saturated B0 baseline (recall 1.000), not an additive component decomposition (the ladder recovers 3 flips at A4)

\subsection{Actionable guidance: minimal provenance vs.\ the full bundle}
\label{sec:guidance}
Whatever the component verdict, the study already supports one design rule:
a mitigation that only \emph{labels} what it cannot verify (provenance
headers, boundary annotations) is a different intervention from one that
\emph{re-frames the task} around the label (system reassertion, retry
loops), and the two should be evaluated --- and shipped --- separately. The
Round-6 ladder operationalizes that split into a keep/drop decision made by
rule, not taste (\texttt{docs/round6\_ablation\_prereg.md}, frozen before
the ablation ran). The branch that fires is G1: \emph{keep the minimal
wrapper, drop the rest}. Boundary labelling alone did not reproduce the harm (1/60 flips, $p=1.0$; the same holds on the Qwen spot check), while the system task-intent reassertion --- the component that reframes the task rather than labelling the input --- carries 28 of the 34 net B0$\to$P3-full flips (exact $p=7.45{\times}10^{-9}$; replicated on C5\_far at 36/59). Ship provenance labels; keep reassertion off for verdict-critical analysis, re-measure it per model, and re-run the paired flip ledger whenever the wrapper changes.

\subsection{Blocking fears are premature at open scale; corruption is the actionable channel}
Across every generation we have collected on this task --- neutral wording,
defensive wording, security-dense task framing, naive contextual stress, and
the content-anchored C5 advisory attack --- there is not a single refusal
event on a vulnerability-analysis record (zero in all 1{,}200 round-5 E0-V2
records and all 473 defence records, consistent with every earlier cell).
Combined with the anti-leakage audit and the inert-evidence counterexample
(the same advisories that fail to block do move verdicts), the honest
conclusion is scoped: at open 2--7B scale, the feared safety-blocking of
vulnerability analysis does not materialize, and the frontier-model question
stays open --- the 2.72$\times$ wording effect of~\cite{campbell2026defensive}
and the RAG-blocking of~\cite{li2026taboorag} were measured on different
(larger, chat-tuned) models. What \emph{is} actionable at this scale is
verdict corruption, from two sources: untrusted context, which biases
verdicts in the direction the text implies (instruction-like content pulls
toward ``benign'', $74.2\%$ flips; risk-charged advisories pull toward
``vulnerable'', up to 25/30 benign flips) --- a false-positive channel with
scanner-noise and triage-flooding consequences rather than
denial-of-service; and the defence layer itself (\S above). The C5 benchmark
--- query-relevant by construction (100/100 own-sink advisories vs.\ 0.2\%
of naive carriers), anti-leakage audited, gates pre-registered --- is our
released instrument for measuring exactly this channel.
Round 7 extends this scoped conclusion along both registered axes. The
blocking null now reaches 2--7B: the 7B replication adds 240 records with
zero refusals. The verdict-bias channel generalizes across the four new CWE
families on the primary model (Granite-3.3-2B, 4/4 families), and the
reassertion harm --- the defence-side risk --- does \emph{not} replicate at
7B (H-R1 branch ABSENT on an unsaturated baseline): on every measurement in
this study, defence-induced verdict corruption is a small-model risk, and
minimal provenance is safe in every measured ladder (3B and 7B). The scope
wording mandated by the pre-registration is therefore ``consistent with harm
diminishing with scale, family confound disclosed'': Qwen2.5-Coder-7B shares
a family with the already-inert Qwen-3B, so this null cannot separate scale
from family inertness, and it is never a claim that the defence is ``safe at
any scale''.

\subsection{Structured recovery can be a refusal-suppression machine}
Our Round-2 pipeline is a cautionary artifact for the over-refusal-mitigation
literature. Every component looked reasonable --- classify intent, reframe as
an authorized task, demand the structured schema, retry until complete ---
and the composition drove unsafe compliance from 0.040 to 1.000 on a model
that refuses 96\% of harmful requests. Schema-forcing retries do not merely
``recover'' refused answers; they can \emph{manufacture compliance}, because
the monitor's schema-completeness criterion is blind to what is being
complied with. The fix we adopted (evaluate the request before the code;
\texttt{REFUSED\_UNSAFE} with no LLM call; never reframe an unsafe request)
makes the safety property pipeline-level by construction; the round-5
side-ledger confirms the gate-first design under the P3 wrapper (30/30 unsafe
prompts blocked with zero LLM calls; a reassertion-only probe produces 30/30
refusals on unsafe prose). Together with P3's verdict corruption, this gives
two independent instances of the same lesson: the mitigation layer is a
first-class risk surface, in both the safety direction (suppression) and the
utility direction (corruption).

\subsection{Isolation is not accuracy}
P1's aggregate improvement against C3 injection (0.742 $\rightarrow$ 0.484)
is real but composite: most of the discordant mass sits on benign-ground-truth
pairs, where holding the clean verdict preserves an error (the vulnerable-only
stratum alone is not significant, $p{=}0.5$). P1 stabilizes behaviour ---
it neither hallucinates refusals nor flips under instruction pressure --- but
it inherits the model's clean-time bias (precision $0.48$--$0.49$, near-zero
true negatives on security-charged arms). For practitioners the message is:
provenance mediation addresses \emph{instruction-following failures}, not
\emph{detection competence}; the latter must come from the model or from the
independent channel (B4), whose realistic-arm numbers (\S\ref{sec:codebert})
show how far that channel is from competence as well.

\subsection{Availability vs.\ integrity}
Across E2/E3, SIUD $\approx 0$ and UAC $\approx 1$ while paired verdicts
moved massively ($p$ from $0.02$ to $10^{-5}$); round 5 sharpens the point ---
P3's harm is \emph{entirely} invisible to availability metrics. We recommend
paired, verdict-level reporting (McNemar on predictions and flip counts
stratified by ground truth, not just UAC deltas) as a default for LLM
security-tool evaluations.

\subsection{How far do the two corruption channels reach? (Round 7)}
\label{sec:round7scope}
Both channels were established on one bench family mix and one model
scale, so Round 7 extends each along exactly one axis under a frozen
pre-registration (\texttt{docs/round7\_prereg.md}): RQ8 moves the C5
advisory to the four executed CWE families (NULL dereference CWE-476,
use-after-free CWE-416, integer overflow CWE-190, information exposure
CWE-200; the pre-registered F-PATH/CWE-22 slot was replaced by CWE-200 in
Amendment-1 before generation) on the benign side; RQ9 moves the reassertion
ladder to the 7--8B class on the byte-identical Round-6 arm. The verdicts
are read by rule, both directions registered in advance, and a
family-dependent or scale-null outcome is reported as a finding with its
registered interpretation, not absorbed into the headline.
CWE scope of the verdict-bias channel:
on the primary model (Granite-3.3-2B) the channel \textsc{generalizes} ---
all four executed families, none in the memory-copy cluster that dominated
the original bench, pass the registered family gate, and the pooled gate
fires far beyond its threshold ($\Delta$FP $0.062\rightarrow0.700$, 51/0
flips). On the headroom-caveated secondary (Llama-3.2-3B) the label is
\textsc{generalizes-pooled-driven}: 2/4 families pass (CWE-416 and CWE-200
driving), printed side by side with the pooled pass and the two per-family
nulls, which are ceiling-limited rather than evidence of absence.
Scale scope of the reassertion-harm channel:
the harm does not replicate at 7B. The minimal ladder lands in the
registered ABSENT branch on an unsaturated baseline (recall 0.475 at A0,
3 harm-direction vs.\ 5 recovery flips, exact $p=0.7266$), minimal
provenance is SAFE (3 flips, $p=1.0$), and the advisory channel itself
remains verdict-active on the benign side (13/0 flips,
$p=2.4\times10^{-4}$) --- what disappears at 7B is the
reassertion-ladder harm, not the advisory effect. Per Amendment-1
\S0b(i) the null is read as \emph{consistent with harm diminishing with
scale, family confound disclosed} (the 7B model is family-matched to the
inert Qwen-3B), alongside the baseline-difficulty caveat (A0 $=0.475$,
far from the saturated 1.000 the 3B arm started from).

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\textbf{Pilot scale.} Every result rests on tens of samples per cell (E0: 20
paired functions/arm; E5/E6: 31--40; E8: 30+30; round-5 subsets: 60+60 with
Granite at 30+30). At E0's $n$, the registered PASS branch is statistically
unattainable --- disclosed up front; our verdict (FAIL, effect size 0) does
not depend on it, but a small non-zero effect could have been missed. E6's
vulnerable-only stratum is underpowered ($p=0.5$ on 2 pairs). We frame all
conclusions as pilot-scale evidence with direction, not magnitudes.
\textbf{Model scale and transfer.} All models are open-weight 2--7B code- or
chat-instruct models on local hardware; the blocking non-transfer verdict is
explicitly model-scale-scoped by pre-registration (0/3 clause) and does not
foreclose blocking on frontier models, whose refusal policies may be stronger
and differently triggered.
The scope is 2--7B but remains \emph{single-model-per-scale}: the 7B rung is
one model (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, bfloat16, no dtype fallback) on one
90-function subset, and its family match to the inert Qwen-3B confounds
scale with family inertness --- ``harm diminishes with scale'' is a
consistent-with reading, not a demonstrated one, and only a cross-family 7B
run (Llama-3.1-8B or a Mixtral-class model) could separate the two. Realized
7B completions were much shorter than the 512-token cap (medians 54/56/98
tokens on A0/A1/A5, range 46--210, measured from record metadata), so
behaviour at the cap is uncharacterized. The frontier/API question is
narrower than at Round 5 but still open.
\textbf{Round-7 CWE scope.} The RQ8 family extension is 4 families x 20
paired benign functions at pilot scale; the family-level rule was chosen as
PRIMARY precisely because per-family power is low, so a null family row is
``not demonstrated at this power'', never ``absence of bias''. The five
pre-registered comparisons (4 family tests + 1 pooled) are reported in full
with no multiple-comparison correction, per the frozen registration.
Family-level power is the binding constraint: at $n{=}20$ the smallest
passing all-one-direction configuration is 6/20 ($p=0.03125$), so Llama's
non-significant rows (CWE-476 at 5/20, $p=0.0625$; CWE-190 with two samples
of headroom above a 0.900 C0 FP) are underpowered, not evidence of absence.
Family selection used a disclosed test-first, valid-fill rule (deficits
filled deterministically from the valid split; the train split is untouched
by this bench), and 22 of the 160 executed bench rows also appear in the
round-2 measurement manifests (16 in round-1) --- a provenance disclosure
that affects no label, no selection seed, and no round-7 number.
Granite's round-5 monitor uses disclosed fallback
thresholds (no calibration fit); the risk is bounded by its 240/240
schema-complete records and the 0.5B smoke probe that shows genuine refusals
are catchable.
\textbf{Defence attribution.} P3 is a bundle ($\geq$5 components); at the
Round-5 result, effects were attributable at defence level only. The
pre-registered Round-6 ladder (\S\ref{sec:rq7b}) now carries component
attribution, but it is a \emph{cumulative} design --- it cannot separate
interactions between components, only steps --- the C0$\times$P3 control
was completed in round-10 (Llama recall $0.967\rightarrow0.233$ without an
advisory, $p=4.8\times10^{-7}$), so ``P3 harms only when an advisory is
present'' is rejected rather than untested, and the round-5 defence cells are
complete (run 360/360 and control 120/120 records for both models). The
recall-collapse claim rests on paired vulnerable verdicts, now including the
clean C0 control.
\textbf{Measurement instrument.} The refusal monitor's original fit conflated
model over-refusal with instrument error (legacy over-refusal 0.587 on Qwen);
round-10 re-fit separates them: monitor false-refusal on silver gold
non-refusals is 0.000 for both models (passes $\leq 0.10$), while Qwen's true
model strong over-refusal is 0.520. An audit recompute from raw outputs
changed a headline E8 number (Llama B0 0.400 $\rightarrow$ 0.033) and
\emph{withdrew} an apparent defence win --- we kept both versions in the
artifact trail. E0's $\Delta$RR$=0$ survives because the error is symmetric
across paired arms.
\textbf{Substrate.} The PrimeVul mirror is v0.1-based ($-13.8\%$ vulnerable
functions vs.\ the paper); the realistic arm covers all 549 mirror-vulnerable
test functions, so absolute values are mirror-specific.
\textbf{Gate scope.} The P2/P3 intent gate is lexical: it blocks 30/30 of our
30 unsafe contrast prompts but only 1/50 paraphrased OR-Bench-toxic prompts
from the scoring half --- the safety-by-construction claim is scoped to
surface-pattern recognition, and model-based gating is required before
deployment claims. The P3 advisory detector is genre-targeted (adaptive to
the advisory style it was built for) and its 0-false-positive result is
corpus-specific.
\textbf{Coverage.} E5/E6 defence cells are single-model (Qwen); E0 and E2/E3
cover all three registered models, with two Granite-specific disclosures: its
refusal-monitor thresholds are fitted in-sample on its own scoring probes (no
held-out calibration half), and it complies with 0.538 of the 13
refuse-expected probes in the neutral arm --- a model-level anomaly reported
as measured (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

\subsection{Responsible-disclosure notes}
All injections are synthetic template texts; no exploit content was generated
beyond benign security vocabulary; unsafe-compliance measurements count
schema-shaped responses on simulated harmful requests and release no harmful
artifacts.
