\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used for defensive vulnerability
analysis, yet recent work reports that safety alignment can refuse legitimate
cyber-defence tasks. Before building mitigation, we pre-registered a
reproduction gate over this phenomenon on vulnerability-analysis tasks with
three open-weight instruct models. The gate \emph{failed}: refusal rates were 0.000 in all
prompt arms, while contrast probes showed the same models refuse frequently but
arm-independently. Under a semantics-preserving untrusted-context benchmark on
PrimeVul, availability was likewise unaffected (usable-answer coverage
$\geq 0.98$); the real threat was \emph{integrity}: instruction-like context
silently flipped verdicts on 74\% of paired samples. A semantic-isolation
defence reduced aggregate injection success to 48\% ($p{=}0.008$) without
helping accuracy, and a naive structured-refusal-recovery pipeline acted as a
refusal-suppression machine (unsafe compliance 1.00), which our revised
gate-first pipeline eliminated. We release a joint refusal--utility--safety
protocol, benchmark, and defences, and discuss negative results as evidence.
\end{abstract}
