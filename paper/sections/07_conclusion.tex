\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether safety-induced blocking interferes with LLM-based
vulnerability analysis, and designed the study so that the answer --- in
either direction --- would be informative. A pre-registered reproduction gate
returned FAIL with effect size exactly 0: all three registered models never
refuse
vulnerability-analysis prompts, though they refuse 8--75\% of benign
probes. The pivot that rule mandated redirected the study to untrusted code
context, where the real hazard lives: benign security-charged context
silently biases verdicts, and instruction-like context flips 74\% of paired
predictions to ``benign'' while every availability metric stays healthy.
Semantic context isolation cuts that injection success to 48\% (aggregate
$p=0.0078$; vulnerable-only $p=0.5$) without improving accuracy, a naive
structured-recovery pipeline suppresses refusal entirely (unsafe compliance
1.00) and a gate-first redesign restores pipeline-level safety by
construction, and a CodeBERT fallback supplies honest, refusal-independent
coverage at realistic difficulty (F1 0.215). The refusal monitor's own
failure modes --- an over-refusal criterion violation and a recompute that
retracted a headline claim --- are documented as part of the contribution:
joint refusal--utility--safety evaluation is only as trustworthy as its
measurement instruments.

Future work, sized beyond a pilot: scale each cell past power thresholds,
replace lexical intent gating with model-based gating measured on paraphrase
adversaries, re-run on the official PrimeVul v1.0 substrate, extend to
repository-level context where provenance mediation has more structure to
exploit, and evaluate whether verdict-stability defences can be trained for
rather than assembled at inference time. All artifacts --- manifests, seeds,
prompts, cached generations, and per-claim source files --- are released so
that every number in this paper can be recomputed from its raw outputs.
