\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether safety alignment interferes with LLM-based vulnerability
analysis, and designed the study so that the answer --- in either direction
--- would be informative. The measurement returned a sharper story than
either side predicted. The feared blocking does not materialize at open
2--3B scale: a pre-registered reproduction gate fails with effect size
exactly 0, and the verdict survives the strongest literature-faithful
escalation we could build --- a content-anchored, query-relevant risk
advisory embedded in the analyzed function itself (100/100 own-sink
relevance vs.\ 0.2\% for naive carriers), pre-registered and audited for
leakage, still produces zero refusals in 1{,}200 paired records across three
models. What the attack does produce is silent verdict corruption toward
``vulnerable'' --- a false-positive channel, not denial of service. And the
strongest utility risk we measured is the mitigation layer itself: a
provenance-based safety wrapper that never refuses, passes every availability
metric, and still collapses Llama's vulnerable recall from 1.000 to
0.367/0.333 (39/60 paired flips, $p\leq3.8\times10^{-6}$) on audited raw
outputs. Semantic isolation cuts instruction-injection success to 48\%
(aggregate $p=0.0078$; vulnerable-only $p=0.5$) without improving accuracy,
an ungated structured-recovery pipeline suppresses refusal entirely (unsafe
compliance 1.00) and a gate-first redesign restores pipeline-level safety by
construction, and a CodeBERT fallback supplies honest, refusal-independent
coverage at realistic difficulty (F1 0.215).

For builders of LLM security tooling the guidance is concrete: do not
engineer against refusal on analysis tasks at this scale --- engineer against
verdict corruption, and require a paired flip ledger from every safety
wrapper before it ships. For researchers, two scoped firsts invite
replication: the first systematic test of query-relevant safety-blocking
transfer to source-code vulnerability analysis at open-model scale, and the
first evidence of defence-induced verdict corruption in this setting; the
frontier-model transfer question and the component-level mechanism behind
P3's harm are the two highest-value open problems.

Future work, sized beyond a pilot: a component ablation of P3 to convert the
defence-risk observation into a mechanism; frontier-model API runs to test
blocking and bias transfer at scale; extending the C5 advisory generator
beyond its 26-sink inventory to further CWE families and repository-level
context; scaling each cell past power thresholds; replacing lexical intent
gating with model-based gating measured on paraphrase adversaries; and
re-running on the official PrimeVul v1.0 substrate. All artifacts ---
manifests, seeds, prompts, cached generations, and per-claim source files ---
are released so that every number in this paper can be recomputed from its
raw outputs.
