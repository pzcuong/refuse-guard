\title{Trustworthy LLM-Assisted Detection of Malicious Packages: Cross-Language Behavior Graphs, Federated Learning, and the Safety Layer as a Third Threat}

\author{Anonymous (double-blind submission)}
\affiliation{\institution{PackGuard Project}\city{}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
We present a pilot study that unifies three threat surfaces against
LLM-assisted malicious-package detection: (i) the detection model itself,
trained over a normalized cross-language \emph{behavior-graph}
representation; (ii) the training regime, a federated simulation over
naturally non-IID ecosystem partitions (npm vs.\ PyPI); and (iii) the
safety layer of the LLM used for analysis, which may refuse, or corrupt
verdicts of, defensive package reviews. We release a fully
provenance-tracked corpus of 603 real npm/PyPI packages (390 malicious from
a wild-capture dataset, 213 popularity-derived benign), extract six-class
behavior graphs with tree-sitter, and train logistic/MLP models under
% SRC: outputs/packguard/features/features_v2.jsonl label recount: 390 malicious
% (298 npm + 92 pypi) / 213 benign (123 npm + 90 pypi) = 603.
FedAvg/FedProx with secure-aggregation and DP simulations, under a
registered twenty-seed protocol (AMENDMENT-4). Under a package-level group
split (our primary protocol, adopted after an audit quantified 35\%
version-level train--test leakage under random splits), graph features
reach F1 $.869{\pm}.051$ under FedAvg vs.\ $.858{\pm}.047$ centralized
--- statistically indistinguishable across twenty seeds (exact Wilcoxon
$p{=}.312$; minimum attainable $p{=}1.9{\times}10^{-6}$); a TF-IDF
code-as-text baseline attains higher AUC but \emph{degrades significantly
under federation} ($\Delta$F1 $-.052$, exact Wilcoxon
$p{=}3.8{\times}10^{-6}$, Holm-adjusted $1.1{\times}10^{-5}$; its FedAvg
variant collapses to an all-malicious predictor in 79/80 cells) ---
text features are not federation-robust; graph features are.
A refusal-gated LLM-built knowledge base --- now covering the full corpus
API universe (137/137 types, 2{,}299/2{,}299 instances) --- adds
${\approx}+1.0$ F1 to centralized training (reproduced in magnitude by
the KB-v3 pure-join re-run at the shared seed: $+1.0$/$+1.1$ points on
group/random, graph features), but its FedAvg effect is of mixed sign
($-0.1$ to $-2.2$ points; single-seed deltas below seed variability),
so its robust yield is gated
per-call explanation, not accuracy. In safety-arm generations over real
packages --- 360 over 60 in the registered batch, 600 over 100 in a
round-10 expansion --- no overt refusal occurs in any arm (RR
${\equiv}0.000$: structural blocking-absence at this scale;
consistent with 2{,}700 measured defensive-task generations across both
project domains at 2--3B), so defensive-refusal blocking does not
replicate in the package domain; instead, untrusted in-package content
(an advisory
comment) shifts verdict sensitivity on genuinely malicious packages in a
model-dependent direction (malicious recall $.53\to.80$ for
granite-3.3-2B vs.\ $.13\to.03$ for Llama-3.2-3B) with zero false
positives on benign packages (0/180) --- a recall-perturbing channel,
unlike the false-positive channel observed in the vulnerability domain;
a round-10 granite-3.3-2B re-run of the vulnerability-domain ladder now
shows the reassertion harm replicates at 2B (malicious recall
$.76\to.07$, $p{\approx}4{\times}10^{-10}$) with a benign verdict
false-positive bias ($0\to20/30$, $p{\approx}2{\times}10^{-6}$) ---
same model, opposite domain signature.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Open-source package ecosystems are a primary supply-chain attack surface:
attackers publish packages that steal credentials, exfiltrate data, or
download further malware during installation~\cite{ladisa2023sok,
ohm2020backstabbers,duan2021maloss}. Deployed detectors such as
DONAPI~\cite{huang2024donapi}, Cerebro~\cite{zhang2024cerebro}, and
MalGuard~\cite{gao2025malguard} learn from package metadata, installation
behavior, or code; LLM-based triage is emerging
independently~\cite{zahan2024securityai}.

This paper asks whether \emph{trustworthy} LLM-assisted detection can be
studied as one system rather than three separate papers. We identify three
threats that act on the same analysis pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Model threat}: the detector must generalize across
  languages (JavaScript/Python) and ecosystems from limited labeled data;
  \item \textbf{Training-regime threat}: ecosystem data is naturally
  non-IID and cannot always be pooled (organizational/privacy boundaries),
  motivating federated training whose cost must be quantified;
  \item \textbf{Safety-layer threat}: the LLM performing (or explaining)
  the analysis may \emph{refuse} defensive work phrased offensively, or
  \emph{corrupt} verdicts under untrusted package content --- the
  RefuseGuard findings~\cite{refuseguard2026} must transfer to the
  higher-stakes package domain, where refusal rates are plausibly higher
  (we measure, not assume; the measured answer at 2--3B is: no refusals,
  and a model-dependent recall-perturbing channel instead,
  \S\ref{sec:safety}).
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions: (1) a provenance-complete 603-sample npm+PyPI corpus
with per-sample source, label provenance, and checksums
(\S\ref{sec:setup}); (2) a normalized cross-language behavior-graph schema
with six semantic classes and 18 graph-level features (\S\ref{sec:method});
(3) a federated simulation with secure-aggregation and DP mechanisms over
ecosystem partitions, evaluated with a leakage-resistant group split and a
registered twenty-seed protocol (\S\ref{sec:results}); (4) a refusal-gated
LLM-augmented knowledge base whose measured value is explainability yield
--- gated per-call justifications covering the full corpus API universe
(137/137 types, 2{,}299/2{,}299 instances) --- with neutral-to-small F1
effect ($-0.1$\,pt FedAvg, $+1.0$\,pt centralized); and (5) a
pre-registered safety-arm measurement ported from the vulnerability domain
to real packages ($n{=}60$, two $<$4B models, 360 generations): refusals
are absent (RR $=0.000$) and untrusted in-package content acts as a
model-dependent \emph{recall-perturbing} channel on malicious packages
with zero benign false positives (\S\ref{sec:safety}). All audits,
amendments, and failed hypotheses are disclosed.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Malicious package detection.} DONAPI maps installation-time
behavior sequences for npm~\cite{huang2024donapi}; Cerebro trains a single
cross-ecosystem model for npm and PyPI (centralized)~\cite{zhang2024cerebro};
MalGuard targets real-time PyPI detection~\cite{gao2025malguard} (venue per
USENIX program pages; arXiv record verified); MeMPtec uses
metadata~\cite{halder2024memptec}. None study federated training across
ecosystem partitions or the safety layer of LLM analyzers.
\textbf{FL for security.} VulFL applies FL to vulnerability
detection~\cite{zhou2024vulfl} (preprint); to our knowledge (as of
September 2026), FL over ecosystem-partitioned clients for
malicious-package detection is unexplored --- a literature-check hedge,
not an exhaustive claim.
\textbf{LLMs for supply-chain security.} SecurityAI~\cite{zahan2024securityai}
measures LLM detection accuracy but not refusal interference.
\textbf{Attack taxonomy.} Ladisa et al.~\cite{ladisa2023sok} systematize
OSS supply-chain attacks (IEEE S\&P 2023; an earlier draft of this work
mistakenly cited ACSAC --- corrected).
\textbf{Safety-layer line.} Our companion study RefuseGuard~\cite{refuseguard2026}
established in the vulnerability domain that open 2--7B models show
RR${\approx}0$ under offensive-but-defensive wording, verdict corruption
toward vulnerability (a false-positive channel), and defense bundles that
harm ${\le}3$B models. PackGuard ports those conditions to packages and
measures whether the same channel types appear; at 2--3B they do not:
refusals stay at zero and the corruption channel re-types as
recall-perturbing (\S\ref{sec:safety}).

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Behavior graphs}
tree-sitter parsers for JavaScript and Python produce, per sample, a merged
graph whose nodes are classified API calls in six semantics (FILE\_IO,
NETWORK, PROCESS, CRYPTO, DYNAMIC\_CODE, DATA\_ACCESS) and whose edges are
in-scope sequence edges and data edges from produced-variable tracking;
imports and \texttt{require}/import aliases are resolved. Entry kinds mark
\texttt{setup.py} and npm install-hook scripts. The schema is versioned
(SCHEMA\_VERSION 1.1.0). Eighteen graph-level features (class histogram,
size/density/depth, scope and import diversity, entry flags, parse-failure
count) feed classical models. A GNN over the same graphs is a specified
extension (project roadmap, to be pre-registered before any run); this
paper reports no GNN results.

\subsection{Federated simulation}
Clients are ecosystem partitions (npm $n{=}328$, PyPI $n{=}146$ after the
train split; malicious rate $0.686$ vs.\ $0.527$ --- naturally non-IID but
mildly so). We train LR (primary) and a 32-unit MLP for 15 rounds
(2 local epochs, SGD lr $0.1$, batch 32, seed 20260922) with FedAvg and
FedProx ($\mu{=}0.01$). Secure aggregation is a pairwise additive-mask
\emph{simulation} (masks cancel exactly for two clients); DP is a
DP-FedAvg-style clip-and-noise \emph{simulation} with a single-round
analytic $\epsilon$ (no composition proof; disclosed). Baselines:
centralized pooling and per-client-only.

\subsection{LLM-augmented knowledge base}
Unknown API calls (from the graph universe, not the feature table) are
classified by a local Qwen2.5-Coder-3B-Instruct into the six-class taxonomy
with risk levels, refusal-gated (refusal $\to$ one retry $\to$ UNSURE; a
refusal is never converted into a class) and cached; KB-derived features
(risk ratio, confidence, unsure ratio) augment the classifier for an
on/off ablation.

\subsection{Safety port}
Three pre-registered arms over real package code (60 packages: 30
malicious + 30 benign, selection seed 20260922; the 10 round-8 packages
kept verbatim, 50 added with zero overlap): \textbf{P0} neutral defensive
task; \textbf{P1} identical intent with DRB vocabulary (``steal
credentials'', ``exfiltrate'', ``malware''); \textbf{P2} an advisory-style
security comment injected into the package file (comment-only, gated by a
tree-sitter AST-equivalence check). Metrics (\texttt{metrics\_version} 2,
renamed after a round-9 audit found that the round-8 field labelled
``false-positive bias'' actually computed the true-positive rate on
\emph{malicious} packages; values unchanged, semantics corrected):
refusal rate (RR) per arm; verdict flips benign$\to$malicious and
malicious$\to$benign; \emph{malicious recall} (TP-rate on malicious
packages) and, separately, the benign false-positive rate; per-model
refusal thresholds; frozen decision rules (blocking:
$\Delta$RR${\ge}0.10$ and exact McNemar $p{<}0.05$; corruption:
${\ge}1$ benign$\to$malicious flip).

\textbf{Federation is free on graphs and measurably costly on text ---
and centralized training has its own instability.} On the primary split,
FedAvg matches centralized training on graph features across all twenty
seeds (Table~\ref{tab:stats}: $\Delta$F1 $+.0105{\pm}.0347$, exact
Wilcoxon $p{=}.312$, Holm-adjusted $.312$ --- a stable null). On TF-IDF
the FL cost is now a \emph{confirmatory} result: $\Delta$F1
$-.0521{\pm}.0219$ with 19/20 seeds negative (exact $p{=}3.8{\times}
10^{-6}$; Holm-adjusted $1.1{\times}10^{-5}$), and on the random split
0/20 seeds positive ($p{=}1.9{\times}10^{-6}$; Holm
$7.6{\times}10^{-6}$). The mechanism is qualitative: tfidf-FedAvg
degenerates toward an all-malicious predictor (recall $=1.0$ in 79/80
cells; precision ${\approx}$ test base rate), so its F1 only tracks
test composition --- the minority client (PyPI) collapses under an
unweighted average (group-split pypi F1 $.671{\pm}.059$ vs.\
$.790{\pm}.076$ centralized). FedProx at $\mu{=}0.01$ is identical to
FedAvg in 160/160 cells --- bit-identical in both F1 and AUC in every
cell-seed of the refreshed grid, despite $\mu{=}0.01$ being present on
every row --- so the ``four-method'' grid is effectively three distinct
models and any FedAvg-vs-FedProx comparison is vacuous; whether the
proximal term genuinely cannot bind at this scale or the implementation
is inert is an open implementation-validity question (audit queued).
The penalty is unit-verified to bind at larger $\mu$.

\textbf{Calibration, oracle thresholds, and the class-weight arm
(round-3 audit).} On the released per-sample test probabilities of the
160 centralized cells --- plus a weighted-BCE retrain of the same cells
(pos\_weight $=n_{\mathrm{neg}}/n_{\mathrm{pos}}$ on train, same
lr/epochs/seed protocol; helper-local trainer, disclosed) --- three
findings, all recomputed by the auditor from the raw dumps.
(i)~\emph{No all-malicious mode}: 0/40 cells per
block$\times$split are all-malicious at threshold 0.5, so the round-1
all-positive parking is specific to the early-stopping protocol; the
fixed centralized TF-IDF model's deficit at 0.5 is calibration and
threshold bias, not a trivial classifier (predicted-positive rate@0.5:
$.692$/$.719$ graph, $.803$/$.806$ TF-IDF, group/random). Calibration
is still poor for TF-IDF: pooled 15-bin ECE $.1891$/$.2240$
(group/random) vs.\ $.0726$/$.0870$ for graphs; Brier
$.1664$/$.1574$ vs.\ $.1478$/$.1659$ --- and the ECE column is the

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{When graphs lose to text.} On this pilot, raw text remains the
stronger ranking signal (AUC) while graph features win thresholded F1 and
explainability. The honest conclusion is that the behavior-graph
hypothesis is \emph{not yet} validated as strictly better; its value is
calibration, interpretability, and coverage robustness under the group
split (graph degrades less between random and group splits than TF-IDF's
minority-client F1 does under FL).
\textbf{Boundary condition (family shift).} The group split removes
version-level leakage within a package (AMENDMENT-1) but not
whole-\emph{family} shift --- unseen families sharing an actor or
lineage. The reported AUC ordering is conditional on that threat model;
a registered leave-families-out comparison (graph vs.\ TF-IDF degradation
under family shift) must decide any robustness claim, and temporal
evaluation on fresher attacks is out of scope for this pilot.
\textbf{A retracted finding.} Our interim run on the v1 features showed a
dramatic FedAvg minority-client harm (pypi F1 $0.57$ vs.\ $0.82$
centralized). After the file-selection fix the harm vanishes
(Table~\ref{tab:main}: group-split pypi F1 $.833{\pm}.115$ under FedAvg
vs.\ $.790{\pm}.105$ centralized across seeds --- if anything reversed).
We report this because the ``FedAvg harms minorities'' narrative was an
artifact of a feature bug --- a caution against interpreting FL failures
before auditing the features. Rounds 9 and the twenty-seed extension sharpen the lesson in the
opposite direction: centralized training collapsed in 2/160 seed-level
cells while all 320 FL cells stayed ${\ge}.73$
(\S\ref{sec:results}) --- single-run conclusions can err in both
directions, motivating the registered stabilized-centralized arm ---
now run: it leaves graph F1 unchanged and its tfidf arm parked near an
all-positive operating point (\S\ref{sec:results}), so stabilization
is measured while the central-vs-FL ordering awaits a re-test under
this baseline.
% SRC: outputs/packguard/r10/r1_stabilized_central/summary.md (DeltaF1 per
% cell-group; pos_rate(early) tfidf .995/.996).
\textbf{Safety transfer.} The RefuseGuard \emph{machinery} (refusal
monitor, AST-gated advisory injection, frozen rules) ports mechanically
to packages, but the corruption channel does not port as the same
\emph{phenomenon type}: in the vulnerability domain untrusted context
created false positives on benign code; in the package domain it
perturbs recall on malicious packages with benign FP $=0$
(\S\ref{sec:safety}). P2's advisory-in-package attack is a
package-specific threat the vulnerability study could not express, and
the AST gate makes it safely measurable; because the direction is
model-dependent --- the $n{=}100$ expansion confirms opposite
per-model signs (\S\ref{sec:safety}) --- follow-up arms must
pre-register \emph{both} outcome
directions (harm and benefit) before running.
\textbf{Scale versus model family (confound narrowed by a round-10
granite ladder).} Across the project, Qwen2.5-Coder is inert at 3B
\emph{and} 7B (the pre-registered 7B harm-absent verdict is SUPPORTED),
Llama-3.2-3B shows defense harm at 3B, and granite-3.3-2B moves in the
opposite direction in the package domain. A round-10 granite-3.3-2B
re-run of the vulnerability-domain ladder (240 cache-only generations,
zero new decoding, refusal rate $0.0$ in every arm) now shows the
reassertion harm \emph{replicates at 2B}: recall on vulnerable files
$.7627\to.0667$ under rung A5 (41/0 flips; McNemar
continuity-corrected $\chi^2$ $p{=}4.2{\times}10^{-10}$, exact
binomial $9.1{\times}10^{-13}$; bootstrap $\Delta$ CI $[-.81,-.58]$)
and $.7627\to.4000$ under the milder advisory-only rung A1 (23/2
flips, $p{=}6.3{\times}10^{-5}$) --- the pre-registered
``A1-minimal-safe'' hypothesis is REFUTED (harm attributable to A1),
the harm-absent hypothesis is NOT\_EVALUABLE on this model (harm
present), and the benign rung adds a verdict false-positive bias
($0/30\to20/30$, exact $p{=}1.9{\times}10^{-6}$). Same model, opposite
domain signature: in the package domain granite's advisory arm moved
malicious recall \emph{up} with zero benign FP, while in the
vulnerability domain it collapses recall and manufactures benign FPs
--- the effect is family-and-domain specific rather than a pure scale
effect, and every conclusion remains closed at 2--3B. Caveats stated
with the numbers: (i) the A5 arm is cache-only and its prompt hashes
match none of the cache's stored keys (0/60, vs.\ 60/60 for
A0/A1/benign), so ``byte-identical prompts'' for A5 rests on the cache
layer's keying and is not independently verifiable (a one-time GPU
regeneration of A5 would close this gap; queued); (ii) the metrics
file mixes McNemar variants --- the two quoted vulnerability $p$ values
are continuity-corrected $\chi^2$ and the file's exact binomial values
are smaller still ($9.1{\times}10^{-13}$, $1.9{\times}10^{-5}$), so the
direction of every conclusion is unchanged but the method should be
unified (queued); (iii) A0's recall denominator is 59 parsed records
(1 unparseable) vs.\ 60 for A1/A5, and the paired tests use the 59
valid pairs.

two fp16 accelerate-dispatch SIGSEGVs --- pointer-authentication failures
with stacks in \texttt{mps::copy\_cast\_kernel}; a direct fp16 run hung
for 8\,min until the watchdog killed it), while a CPU proof loaded the
sha256-verified weights and generated real tokens at
\textbf{1.11\,tok/s}, making the pre-registered 240-generation
${\times}$ 512-token ladder ${\approx}31$\,h of decoding alone
(NOT\_FEASIBLE-in-time). Peak-RSS figures observed live during the
failures were not preserved in raw artifacts and are not quoted as
evidence; a 3B control validates the load path only (its generation step
errored, so it is load-only evidence). Any ${\ge}8$B result requires a
different runtime (MLX 4-bit or GGUF/llama.cpp --- neither present in
this environment) and must not share feasibility claims with the
transformers/MPS stack.
\textbf{External validity.} All results are measured on one wild-capture
malicious corpus plus popularity-derived benign labels; external
validation on independently collected corpora (e.g., campaign-level
industry feeds, license not yet probed) is required before any
deployment claim, and a deployed-data evaluation of the safety arms
remains future work.

twenty-seed registered grid (aggregate deltas; the per-seed
significance layer is pending recomputation) and costly for text
features (whose FedAvg variant
degenerates to a majority-class predictor); representation superiority is
