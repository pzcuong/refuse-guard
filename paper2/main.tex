\begin{abstract}
Large language models (LLMs) increasingly screen open-source packages for
malicious behavior, yet the files these models read are attacker-controlled.
We measure the resulting in-package attack surface and find it
domain- and family-dependent---and closable. A single advisory-style
security comment planted in a real package shifts verdict sensitivity on
genuinely malicious packages in opposite directions per model family
(malicious recall $.667\to.833$ for granite-3.3-2B, $.167\to.042$ for
Llama-3.2-3B), so both directions harm a scanner: one family loses
detections, the other gains detections while starting to flag benign
packages. Refusals are absent throughout (RR${=}0$ across
\pmGenDefTotal\ defensive-task generations at 2--3B), so higher-stakes
defensive content does not trigger safety blocking here. An AST-gated
comment/docstring strip closes the channel where it inflates detection:
on granite-3.3-2B every parseable defended verdict matches the no-attack
baseline ($23/23$), all four advisory-induced verdict changes revert, and
benign false positives stay at zero; on Llama-3.2-3B the strip restores
nothing---the lost detections are byte-identical snippets of one package
family whose own comments the strip removes---a family-dependent defense
cost. On all $38$ gated samples the stripped attack file equals the
stripped original byte-for-byte: the defense is attack removal, not
rephrasing. Beneath these measurements, a behavior-graph detector trained
federated over ecosystem partitions is statistically equivalent to a
strong centralized baseline (pre-registered TOST at $\pm.02$ F1:
$\Delta$F1 $\pmPzGroupGraphDelta$, 90\% CI
$[\pmPzGroupGraphCiLow,\pmPzGroupGraphCiHigh]$) and adds a significant
margin over a trivial structural baseline
($\pmTrivFullGroupDelta/\pmTrivFullRandomDelta$ F1,
$p{\le}\pmTrivFullGroupP$)---a refinement, not a replacement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Open-source package ecosystems are a primary supply-chain attack surface:
attackers publish packages that steal credentials, exfiltrate data, or
download further malware during installation~\cite{ladisa2023sok,
ohm2020backstabbers,duan2021maloss}. Deployed detectors such as
DONAPI~\cite{huang2024donapi}, Cerebro~\cite{zhang2025cerebro}, and
MalGuard~\cite{gao2025malguard} learn from package metadata, installation
behavior, or code, and LLM-based triage is emerging
independently~\cite{zahan2025npm}.

An LLM analyzer reads exactly the artifact the attacker authored. Prior
measurements of LLM safety under adversarial pressure studied
prompt-level interference: refusals of defensively framed tasks and
verdict corruption from untrusted context outside the
code~\cite{refuseguard2026,campbell2026drb}. This paper measures a channel
those designs cannot express: untrusted content \emph{inside} the analyzed
package file, where a planted advisory comment is, at the input layer,
indistinguishable from the reviewer's own note.

The headline finding is a thesis in two parts. First, untrusted in-package
content is a \emph{domain-dependent, family-dependent} attack surface: the
same planted advisory moves malicious-package recall \emph{up} for one
model family (with false positives accruing on benign packages) and
\emph{down} for another (losing detections outright), and its signature
differs from the false-positive channel measured in the vulnerability
domain. Second, the surface is \emph{closable}: stripping comments and
docstrings with an AST-equivalence gate removes the advisory without
touching executable semantics, fully neutralizing the channel on the model
it inflates---while its measured cost on the other model is itself
family-dependent.

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Measurement.} A paired, pre-registered measurement over
  real npm/PyPI packages shows that an advisory comment planted in the
  package perturbs verdict sensitivity on genuinely malicious packages in
  a model-family-dependent direction---recall $.667\to.833$ for
  granite-3.3-2B versus $.167\to.042$ for Llama-3.2-3B---with benign
  false positives absent from the paired measurement and refusals absent
  throughout (RR $=0$ over \pmGenDefTotal\ defensive-task generations at
  2--3B, refuting the higher-stakes-refusal hypothesis in this domain)
  (\S\ref{sec:attack}).
  \item \textbf{Defense.} AST-level sanitization (comment/docstring strip,
  gated by re-parse equivalence) neutralizes the channel where the
  advisory inflates detection: defended verdicts are identical to the
  no-attack baseline on every parseable pair ($23/23$), all four
  advisory-induced verdict changes revert, benign false positives stay
  $0$---and on all $38$ gated samples the stripped attack file is
  byte-identical to the stripped original, so the defense is genuinely
  attack removal. The measured cost on Llama-3.2-3B (no restoration; the
  lost detections are byte-identical snippets of a single package family)
  is reported as a first-class outcome (\S\ref{sec:defense}).
  \item \textbf{Background detector.} The detector beneath the safety
  measurements---behavior-graph features trained federated over ecosystem
  partitions---is statistically \emph{equivalent} to a strong centralized
  baseline (pre-registered TOST at $\pm.02$ F1) and adds a significant
  margin over a trivial structural baseline: a refinement of metadata
  signals, not a replacement (\S\ref{sec:detector}).
\end{enumerate}

Scope is disclosed up front: all conclusions close at 2--3B model scale
under local decoding, two ecosystems, and a pilot corpus of
\pmCorpusTotal\ provenance-tracked packages; the audit trail behind every
number is summarized in \S\ref{sec:threats} and Appendix~\ref{app:audit}.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Malicious package detection.} DONAPI maps installation-time
behavior sequences for npm~\cite{huang2024donapi}; Cerebro trains a single
cross-ecosystem model for npm and PyPI (centralized)~\cite{zhang2025cerebro};
MalGuard targets real-time PyPI detection~\cite{gao2025malguard}; MeMPtec
uses metadata~\cite{halder2024memptec}. None study interference from
content inside the analyzed file, or the safety layer of LLM analyzers.
\textbf{FL for security.} VulFL applies FL to vulnerability
detection~\cite{zhou2024vulfl} (preprint); to our knowledge (as of
September 2026), FL over ecosystem-partitioned clients for
malicious-package detection is unexplored---a literature-check hedge, not
an exhaustive claim.
\textbf{LLMs for supply-chain security.} LLM-based npm
triage~\cite{zahan2025npm} measures LLM detection accuracy but not
in-package interference.
\textbf{Attack taxonomy.} Ladisa et al.~\cite{ladisa2023sok} systematize
OSS supply-chain attacks; the channel studied here (content steering the
defensive analyzer itself) is complementary to their attacker-facing
taxonomy.
\textbf{Safety-layer line.} A prior vulnerability-domain measurement
study~\cite{refuseguard2026} established that open 2--7B models show
RR${\approx}0$ under offensive-but-defensive wording and verdict
corruption toward vulnerability (a false-positive channel); concurrent
work reports a defensive-refusal bias in cyber-defense
settings~\cite{campbell2026drb}. The present study ports those conditions
to packages and adds the in-package arm; the channel that appears is of a
different type (\S\ref{sec:attack}).

\section{Threat Model and Measurement Design}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Attack surface: analyzer arms}\label{sec:arms}
\textbf{Threat model.} The attacker controls the content of a package file
the defender's LLM will analyze. The advisory is not executable: it is a
comment, semantically inert by construction, so any verdict change it
causes is caused by the model's reading of untrusted prose.

\textbf{Arms.} Over real package files we run three pre-registered arms:
\textbf{P0} a neutral defensive task (``review this package for malicious
behavior''); \textbf{P1} identical intent phrased with
defensive-refusal-bias vocabulary~\cite{campbell2026drb} (``steal
credentials'', ``exfiltrate'', ``malware''); \textbf{P2} the neutral task
over the package file with a prepended advisory-style security comment
(comment-only, gated by a tree-sitter AST-equivalence check, so the
injection is verifiably inert). The P1 arm tests prompt-level refusal
pressure; P2 is the in-package surface this paper is about.

\textbf{Metrics.} {\sloppy Refusal rate (RR) per arm; verdict flips
benign$\to$malicious and malicious$\to$benign; \emph{malicious recall}
(true-positive rate on malicious packages) and, separately, the benign
false-positive rate; per-model refusal thresholds (fitted for
Llama-3.2-3B, fallback for granite-3.3-2B; disclosed per record). Frozen
decision rules: blocking is claimed only when $\Delta$RR${\ge}0.10$ with
exact McNemar $p{<}0.05$; corruption for ${\ge}1$ benign$\to$malicious
flip. A refusal is never converted into a verdict. One semantics
correction travels with these metrics (\S\ref{sec:threats}): a quantity
initially labelled ``false-positive bias'' actually computed recall on
malicious packages; every affected value in this paper is reported under
the corrected reading, with benign false positives measured separately.}

\subsection{Defense: AST-gated sanitization}\label{sec:defense-design}
Defense \textbf{D1} removes every tree-sitter comment node (JavaScript and
Python) and Python docstrings from the package file before analysis;
string literals are never removed, so executable behavior evidence is
preserved. Removal is line-aware: the prepended one-line advisory strips
back to the byte-identical original file. A gate requires the stripped
file to re-parse to a canonical AST signature identical to the original's
(the signature prunes exactly the node classes D1 removes); a gate failure
excludes the sample and is disclosed rather than silently dropped. Two
properties make the pairing interpretable. First, \emph{strip equality}:
wherever the strip succeeds, $\mathrm{strip}(\text{attack file}) =
\mathrm{strip}(\text{original file})$ byte-for-byte, so the defended arm
is ``attack removed,'' not a rephrasing. Second, the registered hypotheses
are two-directional: \emph{restoration} (H-D1: defended recall within
$.05$ of the neutral baseline) and \emph{neutralization} (H-D2:
${|}\mathrm{recall}(\text{defended}) -
\mathrm{recall}(\text{neutral}){|\le}.10$), evaluated per model, with
restore-gains and restore-losses counted and reported separately---because
one model's recall \emph{rises} under the attack, a defense could lose the
advisory's spurious gains, and that cost is pre-registered as reportable.

\subsection{Background detector: graphs, federation, corrected
baseline}\label{sec:detector-design}
The detector anchoring the safety measurements is deliberately
conventional: logistic regression over eighteen graph-level features
(six-class API-call histogram, size/density/depth, scope and import
diversity, entry flags, parse-failure count) extracted from tree-sitter
behavior graphs whose nodes are API calls normalized into six semantic
classes (FILE\_IO, NETWORK, PROCESS, CRYPTO, DYNAMIC\_CODE, DATA\_ACCESS)
and whose edges are in-scope sequence and data edges; entry kinds mark
\texttt{setup.py} and npm install-hook scripts (schema versioned,
SCHEMA\_VERSION 1.1.0). A GNN over the same graphs is specified future
work; this paper reports no GNN results.

Training is a federated \emph{simulation} over naturally non-IID
ecosystem partitions (npm $n{=}328$, PyPI $n{=}146$ training rows;
malicious rate $.686$ vs.\ $.527$): FedAvg (size-weighted average of
converged local fits) and FedProx (exact local objective solved by
L-BFGS-B, $\mu$ routed per algorithm and unit-pinned), with secure
aggregation and DP as disclosed simulations. Because an audit found the
original protocol's centralized solver undertrained and its text
featurizer fitted a pooled vocabulary (unavailable under federation), all
federation claims rest on a \emph{registered corrected baseline}:
\emph{strong centralized} = logistic regression (\texttt{lbfgs},
max\_iter $5000$, tol $10^{-6}$) on train-fit standardized features with
$C\in\{.01,.1,1,10\}$ selected by stratified 3-fold CV on training rows
only; \emph{text arm} = a stateless \texttt{HashingVectorizer} ($2^{18}$
features, $L_2$-normalized), so no preprocessing is illegal under
federation. Clients share the train-fit scaler and the CV-selected $C$ (a
central hyperparameter-coordination assumption; no test row enters any
fit). The full protocol history, including what was retracted and why, is
condensed in \S\ref{sec:threats} and Appendix~\ref{app:audit}.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Corpus (\pmCorpusTotal, provenance-tracked).} Malicious:
\pmCorpusMal\ packages from a wild-capture dataset (deterministic stride
sample, seed 20260922; \pmCorpusNpmMal\ npm + \pmCorpusPypiMal\ PyPI
archives with verbatim per-sample class provenance). Benign:
\pmCorpusBen\ popularity-derived packages (\pmCorpusNpmBen\ npm +
\pmCorpusPypiBen\ PyPI), ``assumed benign, not individually audited.''

\textbf{Statistics.} Registered twenty-seed protocol (seeds
20260922--20260941): per-seed exact McNemar (accuracy@0.5) on the paired
FedAvg-vs-centralized comparison, aggregated by two-sided exact Wilcoxon
signed-rank over the twenty per-seed $\Delta$F1, Holm-corrected over the
four split$\times$block comparisons; equivalence by a pre-registered TOST
at margin $\pm.02$ F1 (equivalence iff the 90\% $t$-CI of the mean paired
$\Delta$F1 lies entirely inside the margin; Schuirmann at $\alpha{=}.05$).
At $n{=}20$ the minimum attainable two-sided exact $p$ is
$1.9{\times}10^{-6}$. Standard deviations over seeds are population
($ddof{=}0$). Both registrations (twenty seeds; corrected baseline) were
filed before their respective runs and postdate earlier results at the
same seeds (disclosed). For the safety and defense measurements, samples
are fixed draws (seed 20260922) of real gate-passing packages, all
p-values in those sections are exact paired McNemar, and every one is
descriptive at the realized $n$ (stated where used).

\textbf{Pilot limits.} Simulation-only trust mechanisms; two ecosystems
(no Maven); popularity-derived benign labels; hand-curated API mapping
tables; conclusions close at 2--3B under transformers/MPS decoding.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\textbf{Rule-based SOTA baseline.} We additionally evaluate GuardDog
v3.2.0~\cite{guarddog2026}, a rule-based npm/PyPI scanner (54 source YARA
rules per ecosystem; verdict rule pre-registered: $\geq$1 high-severity
source rule $\Rightarrow$ malicious; score = rule count for ranking),
on all 602 scannable corpus packages (one archive is empty and is
excluded). On the group-split test it scores F1 $.866$ / AUC $.874$ ---
below graph features ($.923$/$.916$ under FedAvg at the same split) and
below the TF-IDF centralized AUC ($.96$) --- and on the leave-cluster-out
draw (threshold $.30$) it is the top-scoring system (F1 $.857$ vs.\
$.735$--$.835$), reversing at threshold $.50$. The fair summary is
\emph{comparable, not superior}: GuardDog is precision-first (17 false
positives over 602 packages) with a recall ceiling --- 25 of its 81
missed malicious packages trigger no rule at all, and 56 more trigger
only medium/low rules. Two disclosures: GuardDog rules and our
wild-capture malicious corpus share the same origin (DataDog), which
plausibly flatters this baseline on the recall side; and the FN analysis
above is descriptive at our pilot scale.

\subsection{Attack: an inert advisory perturbs verdicts in both
directions}\label{sec:attack}

\textbf{No refusals anywhere.} Across all arms, models, and
\pmGenSafeEight\ packages ($\pmGenSafeSixty$ paired generations), not one
generation is refused (exact McNemar vs.\ P0: $p{=}1.0$ everywhere). The

\pmProbeRefusals/\pmProbeRecs\ times---the refusal pathway is functional;
defensive package analysis never triggers it. We therefore read RR
${\equiv}0$ as structural blocking-absence in this domain, refuting (at
these scales and prompt families) the hypothesis that higher-stakes
content raises refusal, and we do not generalize it to ``safety alignment
does not transfer.''

\textbf{The advisory channel is recall-perturbing, not
false-positive-creating---in the paired measurement.} Table~\ref{tab:safety}
shows the registered $n{=}60$ batch. All 11 benign$\to$malicious flips
across arms land on genuinely malicious packages (checked per sample), so
the corruption rule fires as written but measures a \emph{sensitivity
shift on malicious packages}, not wrongful flagging of benign code; benign
false positives are $0/180$ across all arms and models. The direction of
the shift is model-family-dependent: granite-3.3-2B recall
\emph{rises} $.533\to.800$ under P2, while Llama-3.2-3B \emph{falls}
$.133\to.033$ (four detections lost, one gained). Both directions harm a
scanner: the suppressed family misses malicious packages; the inflated
family gains detections it did not earn, and at measurement scale the
inflation is accompanied by false positives (below).

verdicts'' cannot yet be separated from ``advisory content is read as
evidence'' (detail in Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).

\subsection{Federation-robustness under family shift}\label{sec:lco}
The group split removes version-level leakage within a package but not
whole-\emph{family} shift: unseen families sharing an actor or lineage
may behave differently from the training distribution, and the equivalence
result above is conditional on that threat model. To decide the
robustness claim, a registered leave-cluster-out (LCO) evaluation holds
out entire clusters of related samples: each holdout unit is the
connected component of $\{$MinHash Jaccard ${\ge}.30$ over code 3-grams
and package-name patterns$\}$, closed with each member's whole package
(union-find, so no unit straddles a package or a near-duplicate; none of
the 67 multi-version families is split); a randomized ${\sim}20\%$ of
units per label stratum is held out per seed and the remainder is used
for retraining, for the graph and hashing blocks under the same
standardized protocol (registered before any metric was computed). Over
20 seeds $\times$ randomized cluster hold-outs, both representations
degrade jointly: the graph block by $\Delta$F1 $-.0383{\pm}.0675$
(95\% CI $[-.070,-.007]$) and the hashing block by $-.0289{\pm}.0879$
(95\% CI $[-.070,+.012]$), with held-out test shares of 14.3--27.2\% of
samples across seeds (unit sizes vary); the five-feature metadata
baseline above degrades by a similar amount ($\Delta$F1 $-.026$ to
$-.044$ across methods and thresholds), so the shift is not specific to
content features. The paired graph-vs-hashing degradation difference is
$dd{=}{-.0095}{\pm}{.0803}$ (exact paired Wilcoxon signed-rank on
per-seed degradation differences, $p{=}.368$; FedAvg
$-.0139{\pm}.0794$, $p{=}.674$; 95\% CI of $dd$ $[-.047,+.028]$). The
conclusion is two-directional: no robustness ranking between the
representations is supported, and none is claimed. The design has power
${\approx}.75$ to detect a $.05$-F1 degradation differential at
$n{=}20$; the 95\% CI of $dd$ is not contained in the $\pm.02$
equivalence margin used above, so family-shift equivalence at that
margin is not established (it would pass at a margin of $.05$). The
same null holds at a $0.50$ clustering-threshold sensitivity
($dd{=}{-.0033}$ and $-.0121$, $p{=}.985$ and $.430$).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{The surface is domain- and family-dependent.} The advisory channel
does not port from the vulnerability domain as the same phenomenon type:
there, untrusted context created false positives on benign code; here it
perturbs recall on malicious packages, and the per-family direction
flips. A post-hoc granite-3.3-2B re-run of the vulnerability-domain
ladder makes the domain-dependence concrete with one model: recall on
vulnerable files collapses $.7627\to.0667$ under the reassertion rung
($41/0$ flips, $p{\approx}4{\times}10^{-10}$) and a benign rung
manufactures $20/30$ false positives ($p{\approx}1.9{\times}10^{-6}$)---
same model, opposite domain signature from its package-domain behavior
(detail and caveats in Appendix~\ref{app:audit}). Neither domain's
signature predicts the other's; measurements must be made per domain and
per model family.
% SRC: outputs/experiments/r10_granite_ladder/metrics_round9.json (audit
% "verified": recalls 45/59=.7627, 4/60=.0667; flips 41/0; chi2-cc
% p=4.1854e-10; benign 0/30->20/30 exact p=1.9073e-06).

\textbf{Sanitization is necessary and cheap, but not uniformly
sufficient.} D1 costs no model changes and provably removes the attack
(byte-equality of strip outputs), and where the advisory's effect was
inflation it returns the analyzer to baseline exactly. The llama outcome
is the boundary condition: a cue-based detector that keys on the
package's own comments loses those cues to the same strip. Defenses for
LLM package analyzers therefore need a family-aware evaluation---the
strip that closes one family's attack surface is the same strip that
opens another's blind spot at this scale---and future arms should
pre-register both outcome directions, as this protocol did.

\textbf{What the detector layer contributes.} The equivalence result
(\S\ref{sec:detector}) matters operationally: ecosystem-partitioned
federated training is a viable deployment shape for this analyzer at
equivalent accuracy on leakage-resistant splits, so the safety findings
above are not an artifact of a centralized collector. The leave-cluster-out
evaluation (\S\ref{sec:lco}) extends the same question to family shift.

\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}
\textbf{Internal validity: disclosed audit history.} This project ran an
internal audit workflow whose corrections are part of the record, not the
main claims. Four matter for reading the numbers here. (i) The safety
quantity initially labelled ``false-positive bias'' actually computed
recall on malicious packages; the affected values are re-read under the
corrected semantics throughout, with benign false positives measured
separately (all raw records unchanged). (ii) An earlier claim that text
features degrade under federation was retracted: it was an artifact of a
pooled-vocabulary featurizer plus an undertrained solver, and it vanishes
under the corrected baseline (numbers in Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
(iii) An earlier apparent FedAvg minority-client harm disappeared once a
file-selection bug was fixed, and an apparent centralized-training
collapse was re-attributed to solver undertraining ($0/80$ converged
cells under the corrected baseline). (iv) The FedProx $\mu$ routing bug
in the earlier grid was found, fixed, and unit-pinned. Every federation
claim in this paper rests on the pre-registered corrected protocol.
% SRC: audit history condensed; full trail and numbers in Appendix A.

\textbf{Statistical power.} Pilot scale: CI widths ${\pm}.05$--$.08$ F1
on detector cells; the defense pairing realizes $24$ malicious + $14$
benign per model, in which no paired comparison reaches significance
(smallest exact McNemar $p{=}.125$; zero-discordant comparisons are
uninformative), the llama restoration failure rests on one package
family, and the mechanism ablation is a null with low power. Registered
hypotheses that pass at the ``${\ge}1$ of 2 models'' rule (H-D1/H-D2) are
reported per model for exactly this reason.

\textbf{External validity and scale.} One wild-capture malicious corpus
plus popularity-derived benign labels; two ecosystems; all conclusions
close at 2--3B under local decoding---the direction of safety-layer
effects follows model family \emph{and} domain, so no scale
extrapolation is made. Evaluating ${\ge}8$B models is infeasible on this
runtime (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}); the registered three-client fallback
partition differs negligibly from the two-client partition
(Appendix~\ref{app:audit}) and is a same-corpus partition, not a third
ecosystem.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Untrusted in-package content is a domain-dependent, family-dependent
attack surface on LLM package analyzers, and AST-level sanitization
closes it where it matters most: an AST-gated comment/docstring strip
provably removes the advisory (strip-output byte-equality on all $38$
gated samples), fully neutralizes the channel on the model family it
inflates, and its residual cost---no restoration for a cue-based
family---is measured and disclosed rather than averaged away. Refusal is
absent throughout this domain at 2--3B. The detector beneath these
measurements is federation-robust by pre-registered equivalence and a
significant-but-bounded refinement over trivial structure. Limitations
travel with every number: pilot scale, simulation-only trust mechanisms,
two ecosystems, popularity-derived benign labels, self-reported KB
confidence, and a hard 2--3B scale ceiling; the leave-cluster-out
evaluation reported above (\S\ref{sec:lco}) decides the family-shift
robustness claim.

\subsection{Scale ceiling: ${\ge}8$B evaluation is infeasible on this
runtime}
The correct instrument for the scale question (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct,
same family, ${\sim}2.7\times$) failed to load on this stack: four
independent MPS load failures (a bf16 harness stall ${\ge}27$\,min in
weight copy with zero tokens generated; two fp16
accelerate-dispatch SIGSEGVs---pointer-authentication failures with
stacks in \texttt{mps::copy\_cast\_kernel}; a direct fp16 run hung for
8\,min until the watchdog killed it), while a CPU proof loaded the
sha256-verified weights and generated real tokens at $1.11$\,tok/s,
making the registered 240-generation $\times$ 512-token ladder
${\approx}31$\,h of decoding alone (not feasible in time). Peak-RSS
figures observed live during the failures were not preserved in raw
artifacts and are not quoted as evidence; any ${\ge}8$B result requires a
different runtime (MLX 4-bit or GGUF/llama.cpp, neither present here) and
must not share feasibility claims with the transformers/MPS stack.
