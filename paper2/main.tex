\title{Trustworthy LLM-Assisted Detection of Malicious Packages: Cross-Language Behavior Graphs, Federated Learning, and the Safety Layer as a Third Threat}

\author{Anonymous (double-blind submission)}
\affiliation{\institution{Anonymous}\city{}\country{}}

\begin{abstract}
We present a pilot study that unifies three threat surfaces against
LLM-assisted malicious-package detection: (i) the detection model itself,
trained over a normalized cross-language \emph{behavior-graph}
representation; (ii) the training regime, a federated simulation over
naturally non-IID ecosystem partitions (npm vs.\ PyPI); and (iii) the
safety layer of the LLM used for analysis, which may refuse, or corrupt
verdicts of, defensive package reviews. We release a fully
provenance-tracked corpus of \pmCorpusTotal real npm/PyPI packages
(\pmCorpusMal\ malicious from
a wild-capture dataset, \pmCorpusBen\ popularity-derived benign), extract six-class
behavior graphs with tree-sitter, and train logistic/MLP models under
% SRC: outputs/packguard/features/features_v2.jsonl label recount: 390 malicious
% (298 npm + 92 pypi) / 213 benign (123 npm + 90 pypi) = 603.
FedAvg/FedProx with secure-aggregation and DP simulations, under a
registered twenty-seed protocol (AMENDMENT-4), then re-validated under a
pre-registered \emph{corrected baseline} (AMENDMENT-5: a properly
converged, standardized centralized solver; a stateless hashing text
featurizer). Under a package-level group
split (our primary protocol, adopted after an audit quantified 35\%
version-level train--test leakage under random splits), FedAvg is
statistically \emph{equivalent} to the strong centralized baseline at the
pre-registered TOST margin of $\pm$.02 F1 (graph:
$\Delta$F1\,$\pmPzGroupGraphDelta$, 90\% CI
$[\pmPzGroupGraphCiLow,\pmPzGroupGraphCiHigh]$ --- equivalence passes;
the text block passes as well) --- federation carries no measurable cost
where the inference is leakage-resistant; a small centralized advantage
appears only on the leakage-prone secondary random split
($\Delta$F1\,$\pmPzRandomGraphDelta$, Holm $p{=}\pmPzRandomGraphPHolm$).
An earlier claim that text features degrade under federation
\emph{does not survive} this corrected baseline --- it was an artifact of
a pooled-vocabulary featurizer plus an undertrained solver --- and is
retracted: under the corrected protocol, text and graph features are both
federation-robust.
A refusal-gated LLM-built knowledge base --- now covering the full corpus
API universe (137/137 types, 2{,}299/2{,}299 instances) --- adds
${\approx}+1.0$ F1 to centralized training (reproduced in magnitude by
the KB-v3 pure-join re-run at the shared seed: $+1.0$/$+1.1$ points on
group/random, graph features), but its FedAvg effect is of mixed sign
($-0.1$ to $-2.2$ points; single-seed deltas below seed variability),
so its robust yield is gated
per-call explanation, not accuracy. In safety-arm generations over real
packages --- \pmGenSafeSixty\ over \pmGenSafeEight\ in the registered batch, 600 over 100 in a
follow-up expansion --- no overt refusal occurs in any arm (RR
${\equiv}0.000$: structural blocking-absence at this scale;
consistent with \pmGenDefTotal\ measured defensive-task generations across both
project domains at 2--3B), so defensive-refusal blocking does not
replicate in the package domain; instead, untrusted in-package content
(an advisory
comment) shifts verdict sensitivity on genuinely malicious packages in a
model-dependent direction (malicious recall $.53\to.80$ for
granite-3.3-2B vs.\ $.13\to.03$ for Llama-3.2-3B) with zero false

unlike the false-positive channel observed in the vulnerability domain;
a post-hoc granite-3.3-2B re-run of the vulnerability-domain ladder now
shows the reassertion harm replicates at 2B (malicious recall
$.76\to.07$, $p{\approx}4{\times}10^{-10}$) with a benign verdict
false-positive bias ($0\to20/30$, $p{\approx}2{\times}10^{-6}$) ---
same model, opposite domain signature.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Open-source package ecosystems are a primary supply-chain attack surface:
attackers publish packages that steal credentials, exfiltrate data, or
download further malware during installation~\cite{ladisa2023sok,
ohm2020backstabbers,duan2021maloss}. Deployed detectors such as
DONAPI~\cite{huang2024donapi}, Cerebro~\cite{zhang2025cerebro}, and
MalGuard~\cite{gao2025malguard} learn from package metadata, installation
behavior, or code; LLM-based triage is emerging
independently~\cite{zahan2025npm}.

This paper asks whether \emph{trustworthy} LLM-assisted detection can be
studied as one system rather than three separate papers. We identify three
threats that act on the same analysis pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Model threat}: the detector must generalize across
  languages (JavaScript/Python) and ecosystems from limited labeled data;
  \item \textbf{Training-regime threat}: ecosystem data is naturally
  non-IID and cannot always be pooled (organizational/privacy boundaries),
  motivating federated training whose cost must be quantified;
  \item \textbf{Safety-layer threat}: the LLM performing (or explaining)
  the analysis may \emph{refuse} defensive work phrased offensively, or
  \emph{corrupt} verdicts under untrusted package content --- findings
  from a prior vulnerability-domain measurement
  study~\cite{refuseguard2026,campbell2026drb} motivate testing whether
  such interference appears in the
  higher-stakes package domain, where refusal rates are plausibly higher
  (we measure, not assume; the measured answer at 2--3B is: no refusals,
  and a model-dependent recall-perturbing channel instead,
  \S\ref{sec:safety}).
\end{enumerate}

Our contributions: (1) a provenance-complete 603-sample npm+PyPI corpus
with per-sample source, label provenance, and checksums
(\S\ref{sec:setup}); (2) a normalized cross-language behavior-graph schema
with six semantic classes and 18 graph-level features (\S\ref{sec:method});
(3) a federated simulation with secure-aggregation and DP mechanisms over
ecosystem partitions, evaluated with a leakage-resistant group split, a
registered twenty-seed protocol, and a pre-registered corrected-baseline
equivalence test --- TOST at $\pm$.02 F1: FedAvg is equivalent to a
strong centralized baseline on the primary split, with a small
centralized advantage confined to the leakage-prone secondary split
(\S\ref{sec:results}); (4) a refusal-gated
LLM-augmented knowledge base whose measured value is explainability yield
--- gated per-call justifications covering the full corpus API universe
(\pmKbUniverseTypes/\pmKbUniverseTypes\ types, \pmKbUniverseInstances/\pmKbUniverseInstances\ instances) --- with neutral-to-small F1
effect ($-0.1$\,pt FedAvg, $+1.0$\,pt centralized); and (5) a
pre-registered safety-arm measurement ported from the vulnerability domain
to real packages ($n{=}\pmGenSafeEight$, two $<$4B models, \pmGenSafeSixty\ generations): refusals
are absent (RR $=0.000$) and untrusted in-package content acts as a
model-dependent \emph{recall-perturbing} channel on malicious packages
with zero benign false positives (\S\ref{sec:safety}). All audits,
amendments, and failed hypotheses are disclosed.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Malicious package detection.} DONAPI maps installation-time
behavior sequences for npm~\cite{huang2024donapi}; Cerebro trains a single
cross-ecosystem model for npm and PyPI (centralized)~\cite{zhang2025cerebro};
MalGuard targets real-time PyPI detection~\cite{gao2025malguard} (venue per
USENIX program pages; arXiv record verified); MeMPtec uses
metadata~\cite{halder2024memptec}. None study federated training across
ecosystem partitions or the safety layer of LLM analyzers.
\textbf{FL for security.} VulFL applies FL to vulnerability
detection~\cite{zhou2024vulfl} (preprint); to our knowledge (as of
September 2026), FL over ecosystem-partitioned clients for
malicious-package detection is unexplored --- a literature-check hedge,
not an exhaustive claim.
\textbf{LLMs for supply-chain security.} LLM-based npm
triage~\cite{zahan2025npm}
measures LLM detection accuracy but not refusal interference.
\textbf{Attack taxonomy.} Ladisa et al.~\cite{ladisa2023sok} systematize
OSS supply-chain attacks (IEEE S\&P 2023; an earlier draft of this work
mistakenly cited ACSAC --- corrected).
\textbf{Safety-layer line.} A prior vulnerability-domain measurement
study~\cite{refuseguard2026}
established that open 2--7B models show
RR${\approx}0$ under offensive-but-defensive wording, verdict corruption
toward vulnerability (a false-positive channel), and defense bundles that
harm ${\le}3$B models; concurrent work reports a defensive-refusal bias
in cyber-defense settings~\cite{campbell2026drb}. The present study ports
those conditions to packages and
measures whether the same channel types appear; at 2--3B they do not:
refusals stay at zero and the corruption channel re-types as
recall-perturbing (\S\ref{sec:safety}).

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Behavior graphs}
tree-sitter parsers for JavaScript and Python produce, per sample, a merged
graph whose nodes are classified API calls in six semantics (FILE\_IO,
NETWORK, PROCESS, CRYPTO, DYNAMIC\_CODE, DATA\_ACCESS) and whose edges are
in-scope sequence edges and data edges from produced-variable tracking;
imports and \texttt{require}/import aliases are resolved. Entry kinds mark
\texttt{setup.py} and npm install-hook scripts. The schema is versioned
(SCHEMA\_VERSION 1.1.0). Eighteen graph-level features (class histogram,
size/density/depth, scope and import diversity, entry flags, parse-failure
count) feed classical models. A GNN over the same graphs is a specified
extension (project roadmap, to be pre-registered before any run); this
paper reports no GNN results.

\subsection{Federated simulation}
Clients are ecosystem partitions (npm $n{=}328$, PyPI $n{=}146$ after the
train split; malicious rate $0.686$ vs.\ $0.527$ --- naturally non-IID but
mildly so). We train LR (primary) and a 32-unit MLP for 15 rounds
(2 local epochs, SGD lr $0.1$, batch 32, seed 20260922) with FedAvg and
FedProx ($\mu{=}0.01$). Secure aggregation is a pairwise additive-mask
\emph{simulation} (masks cancel exactly for two clients); DP is a
DP-FedAvg-style clip-and-noise \emph{simulation} with a single-round
analytic $\epsilon$ (no composition proof; disclosed). Baselines:
centralized pooling and per-client-only.

\textbf{Corrected baseline (AMENDMENT-5, registered before any run).}
An audit found two baseline defects in the protocol above: the centralized
solver (SGD, lr $0.1$) does not reliably converge on this feature scale,
and the TF-IDF arm fits its vocabulary on the pooled corpus --- a
centralized preprocessing step that is unavailable under federation.
AMENDMENT-5 pre-registers the correction used for all federation claims
here: \emph{strong centralized} = logistic regression (\texttt{lbfgs},
max\_iter $5000$, tol $10^{-6}$) on train-fit standardized features, with
$C\in\{.01,.1,1,10\}$ selected by stratified 3-fold CV on training rows
only; \emph{FedAvg} = converged local fit of the same objective on each
client's standardized share, averaged with client-size weights --- a valid
FedAvg simulation for convex objectives; the round loop still runs and the
aggregate is a fixed point from round 2 in all cells (disclosed);
\emph{FedProx} = the exact local objective (mean log-loss $+$ $L_2$ $+$
$\frac{\mu}{2}\|\theta-\theta_g\|^2$) solved by L-BFGS-B with analytic
gradients, with $\mu$ routed per algorithm (a routing bug that made both
earlier arms silently run FedProx is fixed, unit-pinned, and reproducible
via a legacy flag; \S\ref{sec:discussion}); and the \emph{text arm}
becomes a stateless \texttt{HashingVectorizer} ($2^{18}$ features,
$L_2$-normalized) --- no vocabulary is fitted anywhere, so the featurizer
is legal under federation. Clients share the train-fit scaler and the
CV-selected $C$ (a central hyperparameter-coordination assumption;
training rows only --- no test row is ever touched by any fit).

\subsection{LLM-augmented knowledge base}
Unknown API calls (from the graph universe, not the feature table) are
classified by a local Qwen2.5-Coder-3B-Instruct into the six-class taxonomy
with risk levels, refusal-gated (refusal $\to$ one retry $\to$ UNSURE; a
refusal is never converted into a class) and cached; KB-derived features
(risk ratio, confidence, unsure ratio) augment the classifier for an
on/off ablation.

\subsection{Safety port}
Three pre-registered arms over real package code (60 packages: 30
malicious + 30 benign, selection seed 20260922; the 10 initial-pilot
packages kept verbatim, 50 added with zero overlap): \textbf{P0} neutral defensive
task; \textbf{P1} identical intent with defensive-refusal-bias
vocabulary~\cite{campbell2026drb} (``steal
credentials'', ``exfiltrate'', ``malware''); \textbf{P2} an advisory-style
security comment injected into the package file (comment-only, gated by a
tree-sitter AST-equivalence check). Metrics (\texttt{metrics\_version} 2,
renamed after a follow-up audit found that the initial field labelled
``false-positive bias'' actually computed the true-positive rate on
\emph{malicious} packages; values unchanged, semantics corrected):
refusal rate (RR) per arm; verdict flips benign$\to$malicious and
malicious$\to$benign; \emph{malicious recall} (TP-rate on malicious
packages) and, separately, the benign false-positive rate; per-model
refusal thresholds; frozen decision rules (blocking:
$\Delta$RR${\ge}0.10$ and exact McNemar $p{<}0.05$; corruption:
${\ge}1$ benign$\to$malicious flip).

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Corpus (\pmCorpusTotal, disclosed).} Malicious: DataDog wild-capture dataset,
deterministic stride sample (seed 20260922) of \pmCorpusNpmMal\ npm + \pmCorpusPypiMal\ PyPI archives
with verbatim per-sample class provenance; benign: popularity-ranked npm
registry and PyPI download lists, \pmCorpusNpmBen\ + \pmCorpusPypiBen, ``assumed benign, not
individually audited''. An internal audit found the manifest builder silently
skipped DataDog's flat layout; the fix adds 6 archives (5 single-version
packages + 1 extra archive of an already-selected package), $597\to\pmCorpusTotal$;
the flawed 597 manifest is retained as an audit artifact.

\textbf{Statistics.} Registered twenty-seed protocol (AMENDMENT-4; seeds
20260922--20260941, extending the earlier five-seed registered grid ---
the five original seeds are unchanged and seed 20260922 reproduces the
original single-seed table to four decimals): per-seed exact McNemar
(accuracy@0.5) on the paired FedAvg-vs-centralized comparison, aggregated
by two-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank over the twenty per-seed
$\Delta$F1 (AUC secondary), Holm-corrected over the four registered
comparisons. At $n{=}20$ the minimum attainable two-sided exact $p$ is
$2/2^{20}{\approx}1.9{\times}10^{-6} < .05$: seed-level tests can reach
$\alpha$. Single-seed comparisons additionally report McNemar
(exact when discordant $<25$) and paired bootstrap (10k, seed 20260922)
on per-sample accuracy. Standard deviations over seeds are population
($ddof{=}0$). AMENDMENT-4 was registered before the 20-seed run but after
the 5-seed results were known (disclosed; not double-blind).
AMENDMENT-5 (registered before any corrected-baseline run, with the
twenty-seed grid already known --- disclosed) pre-specifies the
corrected-baseline layer: the strong centralized solver of
\S\ref{sec:method}, a paired TOST equivalence test at margin $\pm .02$ F1
(equivalence iff the 90\% $t$-CI over the twenty paired per-seed
$\Delta$F1 lies entirely inside the margin --- Schuirmann at
$\alpha{=}.05$), exact Wilcoxon $+$ Holm over the four
split$\times$block comparisons, and a FedProx
$\mu\in\{.01,.1,1\}$ sweep restricted to the primary split (descriptive).
\textbf{Pilot limits.} simulation-only FL; two ecosystems (no Maven);
popularity-derived benign labels; hand-curated API mapping tables; the
registered twenty-seed protocol makes seed-level tests attainable, but
the design remains pilot-scale in corpus and client count.

\textbf{The corruption channel re-types as recall-perturbing.} All 11
benign$\to$malicious flips across arms land on genuinely malicious

on malicious packages}, not the wrongful flagging of benign code. The
direction is model-dependent --- undirected: granite-3.3-2B recall
$.533\to.800$ under P2, while Llama-3.2-3B \emph{falls} $.133\to.033$

three). Context thus shifts sensitivity on malicious packages in both
directions without touching benign false positives --- a
\emph{recall-perturbing} channel, unlike the vulnerability domain's
false-positive channel; the initial-batch numbers (``FP-bias'' $.2\to.6$,
granite $1.0$) must be re-read as recall on the 10 malicious packages
($2/10\to3/10\to6/10$; granite $5/5$) with benign FP 0/30.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{When graphs beat, and lose to, cheap signals.} Under the
corrected baseline, raw text ranks higher on the leakage-prone random
split while graph features are within noise of it on the primary split
(Table~\ref{tab:main}); and against a trivial metadata baseline, graphs
add a significant but bounded margin ($+.02$--$.05$ F1,
\S\ref{sec:results}) --- a refinement, not a replacement. The honest
conclusion is that the behavior-graph
hypothesis is \emph{not yet} validated as strictly better; its value is
calibration, interpretability, and coverage robustness under the group
split.
\textbf{Boundary condition (family shift).} The group split removes
version-level leakage within a package (AMENDMENT-1) but not
whole-\emph{family} shift --- unseen families sharing an actor or
lineage. The reported AUC ordering is conditional on that threat model;
a registered leave-families-out comparison (graph vs.\ TF-IDF degradation
under family shift) must decide any robustness claim, and temporal
evaluation on fresher attacks is out of scope for this pilot.
\textbf{Retracted findings.} Our interim run on the v1 features showed a
dramatic FedAvg minority-client harm (pypi F1 $0.57$ vs.\ $0.82$
centralized). After the file-selection fix the harm vanishes
(registered grid: group-split pypi F1 $\pmGraphFedAvgPypi{\pm}\pmGraphFedAvgPypiStd$ under FedAvg
vs.\ $\pmGraphCentralizedPypi{\pm}\pmGraphCentralizedPypiStd$ centralized across seeds --- if anything reversed).
We report this because the ``FedAvg harms minorities'' narrative was an
artifact of a feature bug --- a caution against interpreting FL failures
before auditing the features. The corrected-baseline layer adds two more
entries in the same genre: the ``text degrades under federation'' claim
was an artifact of pooled-vocabulary fitting plus an undertrained solver,
and the apparent centralized-training collapse belonged to the solver,
not to centralized training (\S\ref{sec:results}). Single-run
conclusions can err in both directions --- which is why every federation
claim in this paper now rests on a pre-registered, converged,
twenty-seed comparison (TOST equivalence on the primary split; the small
centralized win on the leakage-prone split disclosed).

% cell-group; pos_rate(early) tfidf .995/.996).
\textbf{Safety transfer.} The prior study's \emph{machinery} (refusal
monitor, AST-gated advisory injection, frozen rules) ports mechanically
to packages, but the corruption channel does not port as the same
\emph{phenomenon type}: in the vulnerability domain untrusted context
created false positives on benign code; in the package domain it
perturbs recall on malicious packages with benign FP $=0$
(\S\ref{sec:safety}). P2's advisory-in-package attack is a
package-specific threat the vulnerability study could not express, and
the AST gate makes it safely measurable; because the direction is
model-dependent --- the $n{=}100$ expansion confirms opposite
per-model signs (\S\ref{sec:safety}) --- follow-up arms must
pre-register \emph{both} outcome
directions (harm and benefit) before running.
\textbf{Scale versus model family (confound narrowed by a post-hoc
granite ladder).} Across the project, Qwen2.5-Coder is inert at 3B
\emph{and} 7B (the pre-registered 7B harm-absent verdict is SUPPORTED),
Llama-3.2-3B shows defense harm at 3B, and granite-3.3-2B moves in the
opposite direction in the package domain. A post-hoc granite-3.3-2B
re-run of the vulnerability-domain ladder (240 cache-only generations,
zero new decoding, refusal rate $0.0$ in every arm) now shows the
reassertion harm \emph{replicates at 2B}: recall on vulnerable files
$.7627\to.0667$ under rung A5 (41/0 flips; McNemar
continuity-corrected $\chi^2$ $p{=}4.2{\times}10^{-10}$, exact
binomial $9.1{\times}10^{-13}$; bootstrap $\Delta$ CI $[-.81,-.58]$)
and $.7627\to.4000$ under the milder advisory-only rung A1 (23/2
flips, $p{=}6.3{\times}10^{-5}$) --- the pre-registered
``A1-minimal-safe'' hypothesis is REFUTED (harm attributable to A1),
the harm-absent hypothesis is NOT\_EVALUABLE on this model (harm
present), and the benign rung adds a verdict false-positive bias
($0/30\to20/30$, exact $p{=}1.9{\times}10^{-6}$). Same model, opposite
domain signature: in the package domain granite's advisory arm moved
malicious recall \emph{up} with zero benign FP, while in the
vulnerability domain it collapses recall and manufactures benign FPs
--- the effect is family-and-domain specific rather than a pure scale
effect, and every conclusion remains closed at 2--3B. Caveats stated
with the numbers: (i) the A5 arm is cache-only and its prompt hashes
match none of the cache's stored keys (0/60, vs.\ 60/60 for
A0/A1/benign), so ``byte-identical prompts'' for A5 rests on the cache
layer's keying and is not independently verifiable (a one-time GPU
regeneration of A5 would close this gap; queued); (ii) the metrics
file mixes McNemar variants --- the two quoted vulnerability $p$ values
are continuity-corrected $\chi^2$ and the file's exact binomial values
are smaller still ($9.1{\times}10^{-13}$, $1.9{\times}10^{-5}$), so the
direction of every conclusion is unchanged but the method should be
unified (queued); (iii) A0's recall denominator is 59 parsed records
(1 unparseable) vs.\ 60 for A1/A5, and the paired tests use the 59
valid pairs.

\textbf{8B feasibility disclosure.} The correct instrument for the scale
question (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, same family, ${\sim}2.7\times$) is
infeasible on this stack: four independent MPS load failures (a bf16
harness stall ${\ge}27$\,min in weight copy with zero tokens generated;
two fp16 accelerate-dispatch SIGSEGVs --- pointer-authentication failures
with stacks in \texttt{mps::copy\_cast\_kernel}; a direct fp16 run hung
for 8\,min until the watchdog killed it), while a CPU proof loaded the
sha256-verified weights and generated real tokens at
\textbf{1.11\,tok/s}, making the pre-registered 240-generation
${\times}$ 512-token ladder ${\approx}31$\,h of decoding alone
(NOT\_FEASIBLE-in-time). Peak-RSS figures observed live during the
failures were not preserved in raw artifacts and are not quoted as
evidence; a 3B control validates the load path only (its generation step
errored, so it is load-only evidence). Any ${\ge}8$B result requires a
different runtime (MLX 4-bit or GGUF/llama.cpp --- neither present in
this environment) and must not share feasibility claims with the
transformers/MPS stack.
\textbf{Threats to validity of the corrected-baseline layer.} Three
disclosed artifacts travel with the correction itself. (i) \emph{FedProx
routing bug}: in the earlier grid, $\mu$ was computed per algorithm but
never passed down to the clients, so both arms silently ran
FedProx($\mu{=}.01$); the earlier ``FedAvg'' rows are re-labeled
accordingly (they remain valid as FedProx($.01$) runs under the earlier
protocol). The fix routes $\mu$ per algorithm and is pinned by unit
tests ($\mu{=}1$ provably differs from FedAvg, $\mu{\to}0$ coincides
with it, and the audited real cell reproduces its earlier row
bit-exactly); a \texttt{legacy\_mu\_routing} flag reproduces the old
behavior exactly for reproducibility.

(ii) \emph{FedAvg-strong is a converged local-fit-then-average
simulation} (a fixed point from round 2 in 80/80 cells), registered as
such in AMENDMENT-5 --- it does not model partially updated local SGD
work. (iii) \emph{Shared preprocessing}: federated clients use the
train-fit scaler and the CV-selected $C$ (a central
hyperparameter-coordination assumption; training rows only --- no test
row enters any fit).
\textbf{External validity.} All results are measured on one wild-capture
malicious corpus plus popularity-derived benign labels; external
validation on independently collected corpora (e.g., campaign-level
industry feeds, license not yet probed) is required before any
deployment claim, and a deployed-data evaluation of the safety arms
remains future work.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}
A \pmCorpusTotal-sample, provenance-complete pilot shows: federated training over
ecosystem partitions is \emph{equivalent} to a strong centralized
baseline on the primary leakage-resistant split (pre-registered TOST at
$\pm .02$ F1: PASS for graph and text features alike), with a small
centralized advantage confined to the leakage-prone random split; an
earlier text-degradation claim was retracted as a baseline artifact
(pooled-vocabulary fitting plus an undertrained solver); representation
ranking is

% retained as audit substrate; stale-inference flag resolved by
% scripts/gen_paper_numbers.py (round 11).
split-dependent; behavior graphs add a statistically significant but
bounded margin over a trivial metadata baseline (refinement, not
replacement); LLM-built knowledge bases are measurable, with a now
complete ($100\%$-coverage) gated API universe whose robust yield is
explanation rather than F1; and the safety-layer third threat is real but
\emph{recall-perturbing} in this domain, with zero refusals at 2--3B.
Limitations: pilot scale (CI widths ${\pm}.05$--$.08$ F1), simulation-only
trust mechanisms, two languages, popularity-derived benign labels,
hand-curated mappings, an LLM-KB whose
confidence is self-reported, and a hard scale ceiling: conclusions close
at 2--3B, the direction of safety-layer effects follows model family
\emph{and} domain (granite-3.3-2B: reassertion harm plus benign verdict
bias in the vulnerability domain, opposite-direction recall shift with
zero benign FP in the package domain), and
${\ge}8$B evaluation requires a different runtime. A Q1-grade system
needs: per-seed inference extended beyond the regenerated paired exact
tests --- effect sizes, bootstrap 95\% CIs, and coverage of the
stabilized arm (still aggregate-only) across both partitions --- a
validation-locked threshold evaluation (the pooled-test
sweep selects thresholds on the pooled test set --- an oracle; the
validation-locked rerun is queued), a KB-entry ablation under twenty
paired seeds (seed-only vs.\ LLM-only vs.\ coverage-matched control;
the KB-v3 gain is single-seed), a unified McNemar method and an A5
provenance closure for the granite ladder (one-time GPU regeneration),
achieved-power and
variance-decomposition analysis (Holm
correction is now applied), a real GNN over the
graphs, a leave-families-out evaluation, mechanism-isolated safety arms
at $n{\ge}100$/model, at least one more ecosystem, audited benign labels,
and a deployed-data evaluation of the safety arms. At the close of the
present run set, the FedProx implementation audit is complete (routing
bug found, fixed, and unit-pinned) and the primary federation comparison
carries pre-registered TOST equivalence; what remains \emph{descriptive
in strength} is the stabilized-centralized arm (aggregate-only), the
single-seed KB-v3 deltas, and the FedProx $\mu$ sweep (registered as
descriptive); the twenty-seed paired exact-test layer over the earlier
grid is regenerated and script-verified
(\texttt{scripts/gen\_paper\_numbers.py}), but until effect sizes with
CIs and the stabilized-arm inference land, all early-vs-fixed evidence
in this paper remains descriptive in strength.
